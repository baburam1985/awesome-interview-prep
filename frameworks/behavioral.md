# Behavioral Interview Framework

Behavioral interviews assess how you act in specific situations and what past experiences reveal about your skills, values, and growth. Use the **STAR** method to structure your stories.

## STAR Method
- **Situation**: Set the scene with enough context for the interviewer to understand the background.
- **Task**: Clarify your role and what you were responsible for.
- **Action**: Describe the specific actions you took, focusing on your contributions, decisions, and behaviors.
- **Result**: Share the outcome, quantifying impact when possible and highlighting learnings.

## Core Themes & Sample Prompts

### Leadership & Ownership
- Times when you led a project or initiative.
- Situations where you took ownership without being asked.
- How you motivated or influenced others.

### Collaboration & Communication
- Working effectively within cross‑functional teams.
- Resolving conflicts or misalignments.
- Communicating complex ideas to non-technical stakeholders.

### Problem Solving & Adaptability
- Handling ambiguous requirements or changing priorities.
- Overcoming unexpected obstacles.
- Adapting processes to improve efficiency.

### Delivering Results
- Achieving or exceeding goals under tight deadlines.
- Driving measurable impact (e.g., performance gains, cost savings).
- Ensuring quality while balancing trade-offs.

### Failure & Learning
- Mistakes or failures and what you learned.
- Receiving feedback and how you incorporated it.
- Turning setbacks into growth opportunities.

## Tips for Effective Behavioral Answers
- Be concise; aim for 1–2 minutes per story.
- Emphasize your decision-making process and why you chose certain actions.
- Quantify results (percentages, metrics, time saved, revenue improved) when possible.
- Reflect on what you would do differently or lessons learned.
- Prepare a diverse set of stories that cover different competencies and roles.
